## Why this is not pre-filled

collaborator. Two things need checking first:

1. **Institutional IP policy.** Work produced in the course of a postgraduate

   university policy governing ownership of student and staff research output.
   Confirm with the department or research office who holds copyright in the
   code, and whether they must approve the licence.

2. **Funder or programme conditions.** The paper declares no specific grant
   funding. If that is accurate, no funder terms apply. If any part of this
   work was supported under a scheme with publication or licensing conditions,
   those take precedence.

## Common choices, for discussion with the copyright holder

| Licence | Effect | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| MIT | Permissive: reuse with attribution, no copyleft | Most academic code releases |
| BSD-3-Clause | As MIT, plus a no-endorsement clause | Where institutional name protection matters |
| Apache-2.0 | Permissive, adds an explicit patent grant | Where patent exposure is a concern |
| GPL-3.0 | Copyleft: derivatives must also be open | Where onward openness is a requirement |
| CC BY 4.0 | Designed for data and documents, not code | Consider for `data/taxonomy/` separately |

Code and data are often licensed separately. If you release the taxonomy
mapping under a data licence and the code under a software licence, say so
explicitly in `README.md` and in `data/README.md`.

## Note on the upstream sources

Whatever licence is chosen applies only to material original to this
repository. It cannot and does not extend to the Hackmageddon or VERIS
datasets, neither of which is redistributed here. See `data/README.md`.
